The problem

Start of semester End of semester

1 in 2 young people who start university in Latin America never finish. They don't disappear overnight: they fade out, week by week, in silence.

Source: Ferreyra, Avitabile, Botero, Haimovich & Urzúa, World Bank, 2017

Why almost no one sees it in time

When the grade drops, it's already too late

By the time the first zero appears, the student has been lost for weeks and the retention conversation arrives when they've already decided to leave.

The LMS records submissions, not effort

Your learning platform knows what was submitted and when. It doesn't know who struggled for three hours with a topic, who stopped opening the material, or who is about to give up.

The blind spot

The same student, two realities.

Your LMS records what gets submitted. Not what it costs to get there.

How it works

From the student's question to the professor's alert.

Three steps. The student just studies as usual; the system does the rest.

STEP 01

The student opens Pharis while working

On Windows or Chrome, it accompanies the student while they do their assignments. It knows the course prompts and materials from their learning platform.

STEP 02

Pharis logs questions, patterns, and signals

Every question, every block, and every study session becomes data. Effort, invisible before, now leaves a trace.

STEP 03

The professor sees who needs attention

Weeks before the first zero, the dashboard flags students who are falling behind. There's still time to intervene.

A tutor that knows their class, not a generic search engine.

Pharis supports the student with the context of their own course and leads them to understand, not to copy.

  • Knows their learning platform assignments and materials

    Answers using the actual course prompt, readings, and calendar, not generic internet results.

  • Available on Windows and Chrome

    Desktop app for Windows or a Chrome extension that requires no installation. The student chooses.

  • Guides without doing the work for them

    Gives hints, asks questions, and reviews their reasoning. It doesn't hand over the completed assignment; it helps the student complete it themselves.

For the professor
